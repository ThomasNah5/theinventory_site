There is a phrase we repeat internally until it becomes reflex: *context is not optional*.

It sounds simple, right?.

## What we mean by context

Context is not just language or currency formatting. It's the full reality in which a product lives:

- What does connectivity look like for this user?
- How do they think about ownership, privacy, money?
- What is the cost of getting it wrong for them?

When you design without asking these questions, you produce something technically correct and practically useless.

## The trap of abstraction

Most software companies optimize for the median user — a user with stable broadband, a modern phone, a bank account, and experience reading dense UI. That user exists everywhere. But in many of the contexts we care about most, they are not the majority.

Designing for abstraction means designing for someone who doesn't actually need your help.

## Context as competitive advantage

Here's the counter-intuitive part: designing with context is not charity. It's the sharpest possible competitive position.

When you understand a market deeply enough to build for its real constraints, you produce something that genuinely cannot be replicated by someone parachuting in from outside. You earn trust — and in markets where trust is scarce, that is everything.

This is our working theory. We're testing it one product at a time.
